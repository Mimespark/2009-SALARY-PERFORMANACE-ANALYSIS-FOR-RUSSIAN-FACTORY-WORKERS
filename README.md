ANALYSIS OBJECTIVE
The objective is to derive insights into workforce efficiency, hazard exposure, and compensation trends to support decision-making and policy formulation.

PROBLEM STATEMENT
The problem the analysis is addressing could be:

Are salaries distributed fairly among different job roles and ranks?
How do salary trends change over time, and what factors influence these trends?
Are there any performance or workplace conditions (e.g., insalubrity) affecting salaries or efficiency?

KEY FINDINGS
The “Roller” profession has the highest total salary (1,500,477.35), followed by “Metal Heater” (1,138,990.59).
Roles such as “Roller Assistant” and “Metal Heater” assistants earn comparatively lower salaries.
Higher-ranked workers receive higher compensation.
Rank 4 employees have the highest salary allocation (1,559,248.41), while lower ranks receive proportionally less.
“Rollers” and “Metal Heaters” face the highest insalubrity risks (1,080 and 1,056, respectively).
Equipment such as “Heating Furnaces” (1,452) and “Pilgrim Mill” (1,272) are the primary contributors to hazardous working conditions.
Rollers” contribute the highest production output (60 recorded instances).
“Metal Heaters” follow closely with 48 recorded production instances.
Monthly salary allocations are consistent across time, indicating stable compensation policies.

RECOMMENDATION
Implement additional safety measures or protocols to reduce health risks associated with high-insalubrity equipment. Provide better protective equipment and increase safety training to reduce risk. Provide better equipment and enhanced quality assessment tools to ensure higher efficiency and accuracy in quality control.
Evaluate performance-based incentives to further motivate and retain workers in high-risk roles. If hazardous working conditions affect productivity or employee well-being, consider hazard pay bonuses for high-risk roles. Provide better protective equipment and safety training.
Consider introducing a dynamic salary adjustment policy to reflect productivity trends more effectively.
Conduct a workforce efficiency analysis to ensure better allocation of human resources for increased productivity.
Introduce modernized machinery or improve operational workflows to increase production capacity.
Introduce more consistent salary structures or performance-based incentives to ensure stable income for employees.
Offer skills development programs to boost the capability of lower-ranked workers, allowing them to contribute more effectively.

CONCLUSION
The highest-paid jobs are also the most dangerous, meaning salaries are based on job risks.
Workers in higher positions always earn more, showing that the company follows a structured pay system.
Unhealthy and unsafe equipment is a big problem for worker safety, and better safety measures are needed.
